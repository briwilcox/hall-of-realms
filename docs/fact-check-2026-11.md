# Fact check, November 2026

In November 2026 every entry in the table was matched to its English Wikipedia article and its Wikidata item, and its start year, end year, language family and successions were compared with theirs. Of the 695 entries, 396 matched an article cleanly and agreed on dates on the first pass, within the tolerance below. The others, every language-family mismatch, and every succession where the chains and Wikidata disagreed were judged one by one against the Wikipedia article, which led to 23 date corrections, 50 new succession links, 7 removed links and 6 new entries. Every change is listed here with the article it rests on.

Two years agree when they differ by no more than max(3, 2% of their distance from 2026): 61 years at 1000 BCE, 11 years at 1500. A start of 3100 BCE means the entry already existed when the timeline begins, and an end of 2026 means it still exists. Language family follows the group that ruled, so a family that differs from the official language's (the Kurdish Ayyubids ruling in Arabic, for example) is not an error.

## Kept as they were

131 date disagreements were definitional: both readings are defensible because they measure different things, such as a kingdom against the modern republic of the same name, or one phase of a culture against the whole. In 37 more, our dates agreed with the article and the Wikidata value was wrong or described something else. 39 of the 41 language-family flags followed the ruling group as intended. Of the successions that Wikidata records and the chains lacked, 69 were left out as partial, contemporary, or already covered through an entry between the two. 4 date questions stayed open because the article contradicts itself or gives only legend. Five proposed corrections were declined because another reading in the same article supports ours (Nobatia, Wari Empire, Slavs, Southern Cushitic herders, Saka), and two family changes were declined because the ruling group's language is disputed (Bahmani Sultanate, Niger).
