An atlas of world history

Hall of Realms

695 states and peoples across 5,125 years, from Early Dynastic Egypt in 3100 BCE to the present.

Each region of the world gets a hall, and each row in a hall follows one line of succession. A bar's color shows the language its rulers spoke. Choosing a year redraws the borders on the map at right in the Map tab and lists everything standing then; selecting anything opens its encyclopedia entry.

Using the exhibit

  • Hovering over the timeline shows the world in that year, and a click on empty space pins it.A tap on empty timeline space pins that year and lists everything standing then.
  • A clickA tap on a bar, a territory or an index entry opens its article, and lines on the timeline link it to its predecessors and successors.
  • Dragging pans the timeline, which coasts on after a quick flick. Pinching, or scrolling over the time axis, zooms it.Swiping sideways pans the timeline, which coasts on after a quick flick, and pinching zooms it.
  • Typing in the search box lists matching entries as the letters arrive.
  • Dragging the globe turns it. Play runs the borders forward through time, and the ‹ and › buttons step through the border maps one at a time.
  • The address follows the view, so a copied link reopens the same entry, years, language filter and theme.

    Sources and notes

    Dates are conventional ranges from standard reference chronologies. Early dates are approximate, and many polities began and ended gradually. A state that ruled across several regions appears in each of their halls. Color follows the ruling group's language (the Mughals count as Turkic, the Qing as Tungusic), so it records who governed; the people living there may have spoken another language entirely. Predecessors and successors come from a curated list of successions covering inheritance, partition, independence and conquest. Each article pairs a short curator's note with facts drawn from the data.

    Map borders come from historical-basemaps by André Ourednik (GPL-3.0): 48 snapshots from 3000 BCE to 2010, simplified for this page. Present-day borders come from Natural Earth (public domain) via world-atlas and show de facto lines as of 2026. The map shows the latest snapshot at or before the chosen year, so between snapshots it lags events. Each territory takes its color from a matching timeline entry or language family; about 1% stays grey as unclassified.

    Hall of Realms is free software under the GPL-3.0. The source, the data pipeline and a guide to sending corrections are on GitHub.
